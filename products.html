Serving Recommendations

Best Serving Temperatures & Slicing Tools

Cheese and cured meats taste best when brought to room temperature before eating. Here is how long to let them rest and what knife to use.

Cheese & Meat Type Ideal Serving Temperature Resting Time (Before Serving) Recommended Knife / Tool
Hard Aged Cheeses (Comté, Manchego) 16°C – 18°C (61°F – 64°F) 45 minutes at room temperature Heavy chef's knife or cheese spade
Soft & Creamy Cheeses (Brie, Brillat-Savarin) 14°C – 16°C (57°F – 61°F) 30 minutes at room temperature Open-blade knife (with holes) to avoid sticking
Blue Cheeses (Roquefort, Gorgonzola) 12°C – 14°C (54°F – 57°F) 20 minutes (keep cool to hold texture) Stainless steel wire cheese cutter
Cured Meats & Ham (Jamón Ibérico, Prosciutto) 22°C – 24°C (72°F – 75°F) Warm sealed pouch in lukewarm water for 2 mins Thin, flexible slicing knife
Help & Care

Shipping & Storage Questions

Everything you need to know about shelf life, cold shipping, and keeping cheeses fresh at home.

Every package is shipped in biodegradable insulated box liners with non-toxic frozen gel packs. This ensures the internal temperature stays below 7°C (45°F) for up to 48 hours, keeping your cheeses and meats perfectly cold and fresh.

In their unopened vacuum packaging, hard cheeses like Comté and Manchego will easily stay fresh for up to 60 days in your refrigerator. Soft cheeses (like Brie and Brillat-Savarin) are best enjoyed within 14 days of opening.
