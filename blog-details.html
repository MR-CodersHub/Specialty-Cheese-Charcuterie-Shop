Inside the Mountain Fort: How 36-Month Aged French Comté is Made

Why an underground stone fortress provides the ideal cool climate for world-class artisan cheese.

By Emile Laurent, Master Affineur • October 3, 2026 • 7 Min Read

Stone galleries inside Fort Saint-Antoine, France. Constant 10°C temperature and 92% natural humidity.

D eep inside the limestone mountains of the French Jura region stands an extraordinary building: Fort Saint-Antoine. Built in 1879 by military engineers as an underground defense fort, its thick stone walls and subterranean tunnels were designed to survive heavy artillery. Today, instead of weapons, these quiet stone vaults house over 100,000 wheels of authentic French Comté cheese.

In 1966, cheesemaker Marcel Petite made a bold decision. While modern dairy factories preferred dry, heated warehouses for rapid sales, Petite realized that true cheese requires natural patience. The natural dampness and cool 10°C air under 25 meters of earth created the exact environment needed to develop complex hazelnut and butter flavors.

Every morning, skilled cellar workers walk down the spruce wood racks carrying small brass sounding hammers. Lightly tapping each wheel reveals its internal consistency. A hollow sound warns of an air pocket, while a clear, ringing tone confirms that the cheese paste is dense, uniform, and maturing into perfection.

What Are Those Crunchy White Crystals?

As high-quality cheese ages past 24 months, natural milk proteins gently break down into amino acids. These amino acids group together and form tiny white crystals called tyrosine. They are completely natural, harmless, and deliver that wonderful slight crunch that cheese connoisseurs love.

Best Wine Pairing: Crisp dry white wines like Chablis or rich medium-bodied red wines.
1966 Cellar Journal Entry

From the Founder's Diary: Fort Saint-Antoine

“The cool air inside the stone fort is remarkable today. The natural spruce shelves are lightly fragrant. We transferred fifty wheels made from high alpine summer milk into Gallery West. The wheels sound clear and resonant upon tapping. We will let them rest quietly through the winter.”
